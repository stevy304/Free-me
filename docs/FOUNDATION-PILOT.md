# Free Me: a foundation to prove

## First product promise

An organizer can broadcast an event, publish its recording, and give the audience one place to return
for the next event. Test this with one community before opening many content categories.
This is a product hypothesis, not a claim of market demand or a list of completed integrations.

For the first pilot, use creators and organizers you can support personally. Ask for permission to
publish their content. They can keep their existing platforms; Free Me must earn repeat use.

## What exists and what still needs proof

| Foundation | Existing implementation | Pilot evidence required |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identity | Email sign-in, channel creation, administrator controls | A new participant signs in and creates a channel without help |
| Publishing | Multipart upload and resume, processing queues, higher-quality upgrades | Interrupt an upload, resume it, finish processing, publish and watch |
| Viewing | Adaptive video player, playback telemetry, search and discovery | Play on target phones and connections; record startup and buffering |
| Broadcasting | Live publishing and recording paths | Run an event, interrupt and reconnect, verify viewers recover and the recording plays |
| Operations | Launch checklist, system status, playback health | Check both processing queues, dependencies, failures, moderation and restore |
| Economics | Capacity calculator and editable assumptions | Compare provider bills and encoding hours with measured viewing and uploads |

An implementation is not evidence of reliability. The launch checklist checks configuration; it does
not certify storage access, end-to-end email delivery, successful encoding, or viewer capacity.

## One real rehearsal

1. Use an organizer account and a separate viewer account on another device.
2. Sign in, create a channel, upload a short video, interrupt the connection, and resume.
3. Confirm a playable rendition appears, then verify the completed higher qualities.
4. Publish the video; verify search/discovery and playback from the viewer account.
5. Change it to private; check that an unauthorized viewer cannot access it.
6. Broadcast a short live session. Interrupt the host connection and check recovery on both devices.
7. End the broadcast and verify the recording. Report a test item and resolve it as a moderator.
8. Restore a staging backup and play the restored media.

Record date, commit, device/network, expected result, observed result, timing, and errors for each step.
Leave failed or untested steps visible. Never fill the record with assumed results.

## Creative direction to test after the rehearsal

**Event becomes a series:** one event page links to the live broadcast, recording, organizer channel,
and next session. A viewer who misses the live event still has a reason to return.

**Creator introduction instead of an empty feed:** a new viewer chooses a few topics and sees a small
set of relevant creators. Existing topic selection is the starting point; measure whether viewers find
something worth watching before building a more complex recommendation system.

**Publish once, prepare a shareable invitation:** help organizers share a clear event link through the
channels their community already uses. This is a proposed organizer workflow, not an integration that
sends messages automatically. No automatic outreach is part of the pilot.

## Decision after repeated events

Count organizers who publish again and viewers who return in a later week. Ask what brought them back,
what failed, and what they would pay for. Distinguish personal recruitment from unprompted return use.
Choose success targets and a spending ceiling before starting; they depend on the community and budget.
If repeat use is weak, change the experience or audience before increasing infrastructure spending.

No 100-million-user readiness claim follows from a successful pilot. The next step is a larger measured
deployment with a funded operating budget.
